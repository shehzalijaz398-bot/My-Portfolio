History of Anime

Anime originated in Japan in the early 20th century, with the first animations appearing around 1917. Early creators were inspired by Western animation, but quickly developed their own unique storytelling style and artistic identity. These early works were short films and experimental animations.

After World War II, anime began to evolve rapidly. One of the most influential figures was Osamu Tezuka, often called the "God of Manga." His works like Astro Boy introduced cinematic techniques, large expressive eyes, and deep emotional storytelling that became a foundation of modern anime.

During the 1980s and 1990s, anime expanded globally with iconic series such as Dragon Ball, Naruto, and many others. This era saw the rise of action, adventure, and sci-fi genres that captivated audiences worldwide.

In the modern era, anime has become a global phenomenon. With streaming platforms and digital production, anime is now more accessible than ever. Popular series like Demon Slayer, Attack on Titan, and Jujutsu Kaisen have achieved massive international success.

Today, anime is not just entertainment—it's a cultural movement influencing art, fashion, gaming, and storytelling worldwide. Millions of fans continue to celebrate anime for its creativity, emotional depth, and limitless imagination.
